I've been meaning to write publicly for a long time.

Not because I have a brand to build or a funnel to fill — I'm skeptical of both. But because some of the most useful ideas I've encountered came from researchers and builders who wrote honestly about what they were working on and what they were confused about.

This is an attempt to do the same.

## What you'll find here

I work at a strange intersection. On one side: technical AI research — multi-agent systems, synthetic data generation, model evaluation. On the other: institutions — governance, student representation, how large organizations actually make decisions and why they so often make the wrong ones.

Most people in AI research don't spend much time in boardrooms. Most people in governance don't think about generative adversarial networks. I've ended up doing both, which means I have a perspective that's occasionally useful and often uncomfortable for audiences on either side.

That discomfort is the point.

I'll write about:

- **AI research** — what I'm working on, what questions feel interesting, what I'm genuinely uncertain about
- **Institutions and governance** — what I learned leading a $29M organization and sitting on a university board, and how it shapes how I think about AI deployment
- **Building companies** — the specific, unglamorous, interesting work of making Arcite-AI useful to organizations that aren't sure they need it yet
- **Synthesis** — the ideas that don't fit neatly into any of those categories

I'll try to write things I'd want to read. That means concrete over abstract, honest over polished, useful over impressive.

No promises on cadence, but I'll aim for once or twice a week.

If something here is useful, wrong, or worth arguing about — [reach out](mailto:temperature6464@gmail.com).
